## [SpaceX S-1 filing reveals $1.25B/month cloud deal with Anthropic for COLOSSUS compute.](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/20/spacex-s1/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 9.0/10

SpaceX's S-1 filing for its IPO revealed that in May 2026, it entered into Cloud Services Agreements with Anthropic PBC, granting the AI company access to compute capacity across the COLOSSUS and COLOSSUS II infrastructure. Under these agreements, Anthropic has agreed to pay SpaceX $1.25 billion per month through May 2029, with a ramp-up period at a reduced fee. This deal signifies a massive, long-term financial commitment that redefines the economics of AI infrastructure, positioning SpaceX's COLOSSUS as a shared, revenue-generating asset for multiple frontier AI labs. The agreement includes a 90-day termination clause for either party and specifies that capacity ramped up in May and June 2026 at a reduced fee. The filing also notes that SpaceX uses the same COLOSSUS II infrastructure to train its proprietary AI applications like Grok 5.

## [Opinion: AI training is unauthorized plagiarism at a massive scale.](https://axelk.ee/ai-is-just-unauthorised-plagiarism-at-a-bigger-scale/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

An opinion piece published online argues that the practice of scraping vast amounts of copyrighted content from the web to train large language models (LLMs) constitutes unauthorized plagiarism on an unprecedented scale. The author questions the ethical and legal foundations of this common industry practice. This argument strikes at the core of the generative AI industry's business model, which relies on massive datasets often collected without explicit permission. Its resolution could reshape copyright law, determine liability for AI companies, and fundamentally alter how content creators are compensated in the digital age. The legal debate centers on whether such data use qualifies as 'fair use,' with recent court rulings showing divergence; one U.S. district court deemed AI training 'quintessentially transformative' fair use, while other cases are pending. Companies commonly use web scraping APIs and public datasets like Common Crawl to assemble training data, a process the piece likens to theft.

hackernews · speckx · May 21, 13:38 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48222383)

**Background**: Large language models (LLMs) like GPT-4 are trained on enormous datasets compiled by scraping text from the internet, including books, articles, and websites. The legal doctrine of 'fair use' in copyright law allows limited use of copyrighted material without permission for purposes such as criticism, comment, or research, but its application to AI training is currently being tested in courts worldwide. Web scraping is a common technical method to automatically extract data from websites for such datasets.

The shift follows stricter U.S. export controls imposed by the Trump administration in April, which require licenses for chip exports to China and have effectively locked NVIDIA out. ## [AMD Launches Ryzen AI Max 400 Series Processors with 192 GB Unified Memory](https://www.techpowerup.com/349218/amd-launches-the-ryzen-ai-max-400-series-processors-strix-halo-gets-a-memory-upgrade) ⭐️ 8.0/10

AMD has launched its Ryzen AI Max 400 series processors, an upgrade to the Strix Halo platform, which raises the unified memory ceiling from 128 GB to 192 GB. The series also features a CPU boost up to 5.2 GHz, a GPU up to 3.0 GHz, and an NPU delivering up to 55 TOPS of AI performance. This significant hardware upgrade directly targets AI developers and system builders by providing a large, unified memory pool that can run large language models with over 300 billion parameters on-device. It represents AMD's strategic push to capture a share of the burgeoning on-device AI and LLM development market, competing with other chipmakers' AI-accelerated platforms. The initial launch includes three PRO commercial models: the flagship 495 with a 16-core CPU and 40CU iGPU, the 490 with 12 cores, and the 485 with 8 cores, both featuring a 32CU iGPU. AMD also plans to release Halo devices pre-configured with this series and 192 GB of memory, with consumer versions expected later this year.

telegram · zaihuapd · May 21, 08:15

**Background**: The Strix Halo is AMD's high-performance APU platform designed for premium laptops, featuring powerful integrated graphics and a dedicated Neural Processing Unit (NPU) for AI workloads. TOPS (Tera Operations Per Second) is a standard metric for measuring the peak inferencing performance of an NPU, indicating how many trillion operations it can perform per second for tasks like running AI models. Unified memory architecture allows the CPU, GPU, and NPU to access a single, shared pool of memory, which reduces data copying overhead and can improve performance for compute-intensive tasks like AI.
